# Changelog

What changed in each release of Amuse, for the people who run it. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) (before 1.0, a minor version may change how things work).

## [Unreleased]

### Upgrade notes
- **Your data moves into one database on first start.** The conversation history, chats, permissions, password, defaults and the index of sent files are imported from the old files into `amuse.db` in the data folder, which is checked against the old files before they are renamed to `<name>.bak` (`events.jsonl.bak`, `chats.json.bak`, …) and kept, never deleted. Nothing to do; delete the `.bak` files once you are happy. The keys, the model choice and the sandbox's credentials go into the Vault (below).
- **Never run an older version of Amuse on this data.** It does not know the database: it would start with no history and no password, and the next visitor could set one. Amuse refuses to start if the old files turn up beside the database again (an interrupted update, or an older version that was run), and its address says what to do; `amuse-admin retire-old-files` finishes an interrupted rename, once the files are exactly what was imported.
- If the move cannot finish, Amuse does not start, and its address shows a page that says which step failed and what to do (the server's log has the details); your data is as it was.
- To go back to the version before this one anyway, after stopping Amuse: delete `amuse.db`, `amuse.db-wal` and `amuse.db-shm`, rename every `*.bak` back to its name (`events.jsonl.bak` → `events.jsonl`, `chats/<id>.jsonl.bak` → `chats/<id>.jsonl`, …), and start the older version. What was said since the update is lost; export it first with `amuse-admin export`. To get back your keys and the sandbox's credentials too, first run `amuse-admin restore-secret-files --yes` (it writes `keys.json` and `sandbox-secrets.json` out again from their sealed copies, which needs the Vault's key), rename `models.json.bak` to `models.json`, and note that the older version has no Vault: what you stored only there is not in it. To undo only the last update's changes to the database, the copy Amuse made before it is in `backups/` (`*-pre-migrate.db`).
- **Forgot the password** is now `docker compose exec amuse amuse-admin reset-password` (deleting `access.json` no longer does anything). Run `amuse-admin` as the container's default user.
- Before an update that changes the database, Amuse copies it to `backups/` in the data folder, and keeps the last five such copies.
- **Set a PIN after the update.** The first time you open Amuse it asks for a 6-digit PIN (with your password); "Later" puts that off for a day, but anything sensitive asks for the PIN to be set first. The devices you were signed in on stay signed in and are trusted; any other device now waits for one of them to approve it. Forgot the PIN: `amuse-admin reset-pin`; lost every device: `amuse-admin reset-devices`. (#30)
- **The PIN is optional, and `amuse-admin reset-pin` leaves it off.** A reset no longer asks for a new PIN on the next visit: the PIN is off until you set one in Settings › Security, where a **Use a PIN** switch turns it on and off (the password, and the PIN there is). (#66)

### Added
- Pictures in the chat open in a proper viewer: pinch or double-tap to zoom (the mouse wheel on a computer) and drag to look around; swipe left or right for the chat's other pictures (the arrow keys on a computer); swipe up or down to close it, the picture following your finger as the black behind it fades (or press Esc, or Back on a phone). Zooming in brings in the full-resolution original. (#83)
- Amuse can offer a few short answers under its message when the next step is one of a few clear choices. Tap one to send it as your reply: it turns solid with a check and the others dim. Once you write something else, they stop being tappable.
- A built-in daily schedule, Tidy terminals, closes the shared terminals nobody is using (idle shells untouched for a day, and the tabs they leave empty), and never one where something is running. Pause or change it in Schedules like any other.
- Amuse closes the terminals it opened for a task once the task is done, keeping only what still runs for you.
- While you dictate, the message box becomes a level meter: bars scroll by as you speak, with the time (`0:07`), a ✕ that throws the recording away and the round stop button, which stops and turns it into text. While it turns into text the stop button spins, and the ✕ still lets you give up if the speech service is slow. If it hears nothing for the first few seconds it says so, and keeps recording. (#50)
- When something goes wrong, Amuse now says so the same way everywhere, with **Retry** where trying again makes sense and **Ask Amuse** (「问 Amuse」) beside it. A message about something that failed (voice not turning into text, a change that did not save, a connection that did not go through) floats above the message box and goes away by itself, staying a little longer when it has buttons. A page that cannot load (Connections, Models, Logins, Vault, Schedules, Skills, Permissions, a goal) shows a card in place of its contents, and a part of a page that failed shows a bar at its top. Tapping **Ask Amuse** sends nothing: it closes Settings, puts "I ran into this — can you take a look?" in the message box (words you had typed stay) with a bar over it that quotes the error, and you add what you like and send. Amuse is told what failed and where (the error code, the feature, the provider and the time, with any key or token left out), which you do not see, and your message shows the error it was about. A mistake in what you typed (a wrong PIN, a bad address) stays as a small line under the field. When Amuse refuses something (it was already stopped, it is not there any more), you now read a short sentence in your language instead of its internal wording; the internal wording goes to Amuse if you ask it. (#70)
- You can reply to anything Amuse says or shows: its messages and its cards (approvals, connections, scheduled tasks, browser tasks, requests for a secret). On a phone, press and hold a message or card and choose **Reply** (or **Copy**); on a computer, hover it and use the reply arrow. A bar over the message box says "Replying to Amuse" with a line of what you are answering and a ✕ to cancel; your message then shows what it answers, and tapping that takes you to the original. Amuse is told what you replied to, so "change this one" is clear even when the original is far back in the chat. (#69)
- You can reply to your own messages too, and the long-press menu on a message (yours or Amuse's) has **Select text**, which selects the message's words right in its bubble, where the phone's own handles and copy menu work; a tap elsewhere ends it. A tap on a message no longer opens a menu: press and hold does, and on a computer selecting text with the mouse works as before. (#69)
- When Amuse sets up a scheduled task, the chat shows a card for it: its title, "Scheduled task", what it does and when, and a **View scheduled task** button that opens Settings › Schedules. There is one card per task, and it stays up to date: it says when the task is paused, running, done or cancelled, and a task that has ended no longer offers the button. (#68)
- The PIN is optional: Settings › Security has a **Use a PIN** switch beside the password, and the first prompt that asks for a PIN has a "Don't use a PIN" link. Turning it off says what it costs (whoever has your signed-in session can open the Vault and loosen permissions), then takes the password, and the PIN there is. With it off, sensitive actions go through without asking and no reminder comes back; setting a PIN again restores all of it. (#66)
- Amuse can show you a page and keep what a page shows. When you ask to see something, or a task ends with something worth keeping (a confirmation, a score, a certificate), it saves a picture of the page under `screenshots/` and sends it in the chat; a browser task can save a picture of the whole page or of exactly one element (a table, a chart, a certificate panel) and say where it is. A browser task can also keep an image a page shows — a gallery, a photo, a floor plan — under `images/`, instead of hunting for the site's download button: what is kept is the picture as the page shows it, at the size it shows it, since the browser cannot fetch the file the page holds. Saving any of these needs no approval: nothing leaves your machine, and downloads the site itself makes keep the permission they had. (#67)

- A command that takes longer than 10 seconds no longer holds Amuse up: it carries on in the background (up to 30 minutes, 4 at a time per chat) and Amuse tells you when it ends, even if it is busy with something else or the chat is quiet, and can look at its output or stop it. Stopping Amuse, resetting its computer or restarting it ends these commands, and Amuse says so. Servers, watchers and anything that asks questions still go in a terminal. (#49)
- A browser task that uses up its 40 steps pauses instead of ending: it hands Amuse a short progress report and holds the browser where it is, and Amuse either tells it what to do next (it gets another 40 steps, in the same browser, with everything it remembers) or stops it. A pause nobody answers within 30 minutes ends by itself, with the report kept as the result. The browser's card shows nothing of the pause.
- Closing a browser whose task is still running asks first, with the task's name ("Stop the task and close this browser?"); one that is idle closes at once. Amuse hears about every change to a browser task, each once: it started, it needs you, you took the browser over, you handed it back, you stopped it or closed its browser, it finished, or it failed. Your own stop or close does not wake Amuse for nothing: it reads that with its next turn.
- Every value on a Vault entry's page has a copy button, and every secret an eye to show it. Showing or copying a secret asks the server for that one value (`secrets.reveal`), which needs the PIN like the rest of the Vault (no extra prompt while it is unlocked), is refused for a provider sign-in's tokens, and is not something the agent can do. A shown secret hides again after 30 seconds, and a copied one is cleared from the clipboard after 60 seconds if it is still what was copied (where the browser allows it).
- What the server keeps (the history, chats, permissions, password and the rest) is in one database file: a crash can no longer leave several files half written, Amuse's memory use grows with what your chats hold, not with every event ever recorded (the history is read as it is replayed, not held as a file), and the file can be copied while Amuse runs.
- `amuse-admin`, a command on the server: list what an update will change before it runs (`migrate --dry-run`), export a chat's history as JSONL (`export`), and reset the password. A chat deleted while Amuse is still working in it no longer stops the server.
- A Vault (Settings › Vault) for the long-term secrets Amuse uses for you: API keys, tokens, passwords and key files such as a Google service account's JSON. Each entry has plain fields you and Amuse can see (an account id, a username), secret fields that are encrypted and shown back only to you, behind the PIN (or replaced), and a note. Where a secret goes is set apart from what it is: a variable or file in the sandbox, or one of Amuse's own services. Two limits to know: Amuse masks a secret shorter than 6 characters nowhere (it would mask ordinary words), and a URL or host kept as a plain field is not told from a secret: put anything that grants access in a secret field.
- Amuse looks in the Vault before it asks you for a key (`list_secrets`), and asks on a card in the chat: a long-term secret to keep (Amuse fills in the details it can read, such as a username, and shows them to you; everything you type on the card is kept encrypted and Amuse never sees it), or a temporary login or verification code that is typed straight into the terminal waiting for it and kept nowhere. The first time Amuse wants an existing secret in the sandbox, it waits for your Allow or Deny; a second name for it needs nothing. Secrets in the sandbox are masked as `[secret: …]` in what Amuse sees, including what it reads of your files, and each secret shows when and where it was last used (and only the end of a long one, never of a short one).
- When its work needs a model provider's or Composio's key that is not set up, Amuse asks for it on a card in the chat that opens the same sign-in as Settings, instead of sending you there.
- Amuse can write to you in the middle of long work: it answers a message you send while it is busy, and says what it is starting before something that takes a while, instead of going quiet until it is done.
- The browser can run on the chat's model ("Same as chat", now the default), as background work already could; Settings says so when the chat's model cannot see pictures.
- On a phone, swipe to open and close the sidebar and the work computer: right on the chat opens the sidebar (more readily from the left edge), left opens the viewer the laptop button opens, and the opposite swipe closes whichever is open, anywhere on it, a desktop you are only watching and the terminal included. The page follows your finger and settles open or closed by how far it came and how fast you let go, on the same curve as the buttons (it snaps with reduced motion). Vertical scrolling is never taken over, and the gesture stays out of sheets, text fields, selected text (the terminal's too), code blocks that can still scroll sideways, and the screen edges your phone uses for Back. While you control the desktop it keeps every touch for itself, and the viewer closes by a swipe from its left edge strip, its header or its switch. On a phone the open sidebar's button moves to its top right as before and turns into the round close X the viewer and sheets have (it was a collapse icon). (#43)
- Connections: services that take your own key instead of Composio's login (Cloudflare wants an API token) can now be connected. Amuse reads how the service signs in; for a key it shows one field, named as the provider names it, in Settings › Connections and in the Connect card. The key goes from your browser through the server to Composio, which holds it and checks it with the provider: Amuse stores it nowhere (no file, database or log), and the agent never sees it and cannot type it for you. (#46)
- Sign in to a model provider with your account instead of an API key, where the provider offers it: OpenAI (Sign in with ChatGPT), Claude, GitHub Copilot, OpenRouter, xAI, Kimi For Coding and Meta. The key sheet asks which way, then walks you through it: open the provider's sign-in page, and paste back the code it shows (Claude), or the full address of the page it ends on, which does not load (ChatGPT, OpenRouter); or enter the code shown in the sheet on the provider's page, and the sheet moves on by itself (Copilot, xAI, Kimi, Meta). The tokens stay on the server with your keys and are refreshed there; Sign out forgets them. Using a ChatGPT or Claude subscription from an app that is not the provider's own may go against its terms and get the account limited, and the sheet says so: an API key is the dependable choice. (#34)
- The key sheet says where to get each provider's key: sign in at its site, open its API key page (both linked), create a key and paste it, and what more it takes (credit before a key works, a mainland China account, a region of its own, a paid plan, or several values with a link to its setup guide). (#34)
- Amuse answers about a task or browser run from what it is doing now, not from what it said earlier: what is running comes with every turn (each run's title, whether it is running, waiting for you or under your control, what it is doing and how long ago that changed), taking a browser over or handing it back is passed on to it without waking it for nothing, and it can look at the page a browser task is on without touching it. Asking a browser that is already busy for something new is refused plainly, and a browser run tells Amuse about its own milestones as they happen. (#54)

- A 6-digit PIN authorizes sensitive actions: loosening a permission (allowing every site by default included), "Always allow" on a card, setting up a model, voice or Composio key or a speech server's address, taking a credential off the sandbox, storing, replacing or destroying a secret in the Vault or pointing one somewhere new, approving or removing a device, and changing the password or the PIN. The server checks it for every such action, so no client can skip it. An unlock lasts 5 minutes after the last sensitive action (1, 15 or 30 in Settings › Security), 30 minutes at most, and ends when the app goes to the background or you tap Lock now. Five wrong tries lock it for 15 minutes, then twice as long each time; the PIN is kept as a slow hash only. A fresh install sets it with the password. (#30)
- Reminders like Signal's: Amuse asks you to set a PIN when there is none, and to enter it once after a week without, so it is not forgotten. "Later" puts either off for a day, on every device.
- A new device needs approval: the right password on it only waits, until a device already signed in approves it on a card that shows what it is, its address and when (with the PIN). Settings › Security lists the devices and removes one, which signs it out. (#30)
- A Chrome policy blocks Amuse's own address in its own browsers, as one extra layer: they do not send the `X-Amuse-Agent` mark yet (their pages run in Playwright contexts, where an extension cannot add it), so a browser that reaches Amuse anyway meets only the password and the device approval. The server refuses every request carrying that mark, the way in included, ready for when the browsers send it. (#30)
- After an update, Amuse tells you what you can do now: it reads what the new version added and changed (the operator-only entries left out) and says it in one short message, in your language, picking what matters to you. Once, then never again: a restart with nothing new says nothing, and a fresh install says nothing at all. (#64)

### Changed
- Pictures in the chat load fast and never draw line by line: each has its place and a soft blurred preview from the start, and comes into focus once it has fully loaded. The chat fetches a copy sized for your screen instead of the original, so a phone photo of several megabytes comes down as a copy of a hundred kilobytes or so. Pictures sent before this update still load the smaller copy, without the blurred preview. (#83)
- On a phone, a sheet that comes up from the bottom (a form, a key, a confirmation) closes when you pull it down by its handle or anywhere on it, or flick it down; a short pull springs back.
- Voice no longer offers Fireworks: it has retired the speech-to-text service Amuse called. OpenAI, Groq or your own server remain, and an instance whose speech service was Fireworks falls back to the first of them with a key (or asks, if there is none).
- A secret shown on its Vault page stays on one line, so the page does not jump; a long one scrolls sideways.
- On a phone, the message box no longer shows a scrollbar when what you type runs long: it scrolls under your finger, and its fade still shows there is more.
- Amuse's replies and its cards share one width: a reply is as wide as its text, a card fills it, and both stop at the same right edge, short of the column's (a gap of 48px or more on a phone, as your own messages leave one on the left; on a desktop at most 600px). Cards, which were 260–340px wide, now fill that width on a larger screen. (#59)
- An approval card is short: what Amuse wants to do, which app or site, why, and the buttons. The raw parameters it used to list under the reason (Owner, Repo, Path, Message, Sha, Branch…) are behind a quiet "Details" that opens them in place, one label over its value as in the Vault; a long reason is cut to three lines with "more" instead of scrolling inside the card. (#60)
- The 6-digit PIN is entered in six boxes, one digit each, shown as dots, wherever it is asked for: unlocking a sensitive action, setting one, typing it again, changing it (the current one and the new one), the weekly reminder, and approving a device. Pasting fills it, a phone's code autofill fills it, the sixth digit sends it (setting a PIN moves on to the field it is typed again in), and a wrong PIN shakes the boxes and clears them. (#30)
- The Vault reads like a phone's password manager. The list is one line per entry ("Amuse's computer: $GH_TOKEN", "OpenAI models", "Not used yet"), with a search only when there are many. An entry's page is read-only: a small label over each value, where it is used said plainly, the note last, and one ⋯ button in the title bar with Edit and Remove (a sign-in has only "Remove and sign out"). A new entry starts from what it is (API key, Website login or Custom) and shows only its few boxes; Edit is the same boxes, with where it is used under a collapsed Advanced. Fields with custom names, several secrets in one entry and file values are gone from the screens: a service account's JSON is pasted as text. Entries you already have with files still show and work, and nothing is migrated.
- The Vault says "Amuse's computer" where it said "sandbox".
- Changing the password and locking a device moved from Settings › General to the new Settings › Security.
- On a service's page in Settings › Connections, the permission rows no longer name the service: "Read content", "Change content" and "Delete or cancel", where they read "Read Cloudflare content" and so on. The page is already about that service, and a name like "Cloudflare Api Key" made the rows awkward. The section headings above them are unchanged.
- Long chats cost less and answer sooner: each turn is laid out so the model's provider can reuse what it already read of the chat, and the conversation Amuse is shown changes at its end instead of from its start (older parts move to a shorter form a few thousand characters at a time, not with every message). (#37)
- Credentials given to the sandbox, the model, speech and Connections keys, and the model choice now live in the Vault, in the database, encrypted with a key kept apart from your data (see the Upgrade notes: back that key up). They move over once on the first start, and the files that held secrets in the open are deleted, not kept as backups.
- Settings › Computer no longer lists credentials: they are in Settings › Vault. The "Saved logins" section is now "Browser data": each site's cookies and storage that keep you signed in (no passwords).
- Settings › Models gives each role (chat, browser, background) a group of its own, with a row for its provider, its model and its thinking, each named; the provider is chosen first, so the same model from two providers is no longer listed twice. The thinking row is always there, and says when a model has to be chosen first or does not think.
- Sheets close with the same round button as the rest of the app, and a task's details open in a wider sheet.
- Buttons with words look the same everywhere: one height for a button in a row or card and one for a sheet's, one way to show it is disabled, pressed or focused. A destructive action in a row (Sign out, Delete, Remove, Reset) is the grey pill with red text that Schedules had, so Settings › Saved logins and Computer match it, and "are you sure?" sheets are all one design. The buttons on Amuse's cards are a little smaller than before, and a connection card that is already connected or dismissed shows that as text instead of a greyed button.
- The terminal's lines are a little taller, so Chinese characters are no longer cut off at the top.
- Amuse's machine is called its computer wherever you see it: Settings › Sandbox is Settings › Computer (「电脑」 in Chinese, where the space is tight), its page says what the computer is for, and the terminal's own messages say the same. Code, the system prompt, the tools and the skills still say sandbox, and the Vault already said "Amuse's computer". The one-time row saying the sandbox's credentials had moved to the Vault is gone: the Vault is in Settings itself. Resetting now shows what it will delete: the folders in its home, outside your files, that a reset would wipe, a folder holding a `.git` marked as a code repository, with a line suggesting you ask Amuse to move them into your files first, and nothing extra when there are none. Amuse is also told to keep anything you may want to keep — code, projects, documents, output — in the workspace, `projects/` for instance, leaving its home folder to tools, configs and caches.

### Fixed
- The Android app no longer asks before every dictation whether the server may use the microphone: an Allow is remembered for that server (another server, or Not now, asks again).
- A one-time code could not be typed into a terminal of Amuse's computer once it had more than nine workspaces (their names go on in letters, wA, wB…). Amuse now also asks for a password a program prompts for (sudo, ssh) as a one-time entry, not one to keep in the Vault.
- Android app: light or dark follows the phone, for the app's own bar and for the web app when its appearance is set to match the system.
- Android app: a file picked with your phone's own picker reaches the message box (pickers that allow several files hand them back in a way the app did not read), and the chat stays in sight behind the picker instead of vanishing.
- A working key is no longer reported as failing because the first model in Pi's catalogue has been retired by the provider (Groq's `llama-3.1-8b-instant`, which answers 404): a provider whose API is OpenAI-style is tried on its own list of models (`GET {baseUrl}/models`) with the key, where 200 means the key works and 401/403 means it does not, and a key bought for speech is no longer judged by a chat model. Where a completion is still the test, the chosen model and the next couple the provider offers are tried, and a model that is gone is said as "the key works, but the model is gone" rather than as a key that does not work.
- In the Android app, opening a sheet in Settings (a toolkit that needs a key, the Composio key row, a PIN prompt) dimmed the page with no sheet to be seen, and the settings screen was empty on a tablet or in landscape: the WebView reports a height of 0 for the viewport units (`vh`, `dvh`), so `max-h-[92vh]` on a sheet and `h-[72vh]` on the settings screen both computed to 0 and collapsed. A sheet now takes the taller of 92vh and 92% of the full-height layer it sits in, which is the same height in a browser, and the settings screen the same for 72vh. (#71)
- In the Android app, choosing a file to attach ended the session: the system picker removed the session cookie, and nothing resumed the picker when device unlock was on, so the app came back to the lock screen; a second unlock could also rebuild the page and lose the file that had just been picked. The picker now keeps the session, always resumes it, and a WebView that restarts (a low-memory kill while the picker is open, most often) reloads the page with the session cookie intact instead of clearing it. (#71)
- Closing a browser that had already stopped no longer shows an error ("cannot stop from stopped"): it is simply closed.
- A dropped connection (a server restart, a phone switching apps) no longer shows a raw "the connection dropped" on the settings pages: a call that fails only because the connection dropped waits for it to come back and is sent once more, so the page never sees the drop, except for what must not go twice (sending a message, answering a card, starting a task), which says "The server cannot be reached." in the app's language instead; and Settings › Permissions clears that line itself when the connection returns. (#65)
- A server that starts before the sandbox is reachable (a deploy recreating both, a sandbox restart, a reset while the server runs) no longer leaves the sandbox without the credentials the Vault keeps for it: a delivery that fails because the sandbox cannot be reached is tried again by itself, waiting 2 seconds at first and twice as long each time up to a minute, until everything has gone through, and one line in the log says what the sandbox was given again (names only, never a value). (#63)
- A browser whose page was closed (the tab closed during a takeover, a reload, a crash) is taken up again instead of failing over and over: Amuse uses another page of the same browser if there is one, or opens a new one in it at the last address, so the cookies and the sign-in stay and the task carries on (the browser agent is told in a line). If the whole browser is gone, the task ends as lost and Amuse hears it, instead of calling a dead page until it gives up.
- A browser node that restarted under a running task no longer leaves the task and its card's preview calling a browser that is gone: its session still answers, but with no page left, so opening one fails ("Browser closed") — that failure is now the same "the browser is lost" as the rest, and the task ends as a failure with Amuse told once, instead of the agent and the preview retrying some twenty times over a minute. (#58)
- A phone's browser freezes a page in the background, and the connection with it: coming back to Settings › Connections (to paste a token copied from another app, say) reconnects at once instead of waiting out a retry that was frozen too, asks again for what the page shows, and lets go of the failure that was the connection; a failure is now said in the app's words ("The server cannot be reached.") instead of the transport's own "disconnected", and a call made after coming back (Connect with a token) goes over the new connection.
- Connections: a service that takes credentials of your own can ask for several of them — Cloudflare's own toolkit wants its global API key and the account email, and many others want a key plus a subdomain, a tenant or an account id — so its card has one field per thing the provider asks for, labelled with what it calls it ("Cloudflare Global API Key", "Cloudflare Account Email"): a secret is typed as a password, an email, an id or a subdomain plainly, and all of them go to Composio together. Before, only a toolkit asking for exactly one field could be connected at all. (#45, #46)
- A bearer token is connected like an API key: one secret field, named as the provider names it, instead of "credentials of your own that Amuse cannot set up". (#46)
- Settings › Connections offers only what Amuse can actually connect: a service Composio hosts the login for, or one that takes a key or a token Amuse can ask for on a card. Everything else — your own OAuth app, the MCP toolkits that sign in with DCR_OAUTH, basic auth — was listed and then, on opening it, said it could not be connected here; now it is left out of the list and the search, decided from the list's own auth schemes, so no request per service. A service whose schemes the list does not carry at all still shows, and its schemes (mode and field names only, never a value) are written to the server's log once, so a wrong guess cannot quietly hide a service that works. 237 of Composio's 1645 toolkits are left out this way. (#45, #46)
- An error about one service no longer follows you back to the list: a service's page could not be read ("Grafana Cloud MCP needs credentials of your own…") and the list showed that line after you went back, because the list and the page shared one error. The list now shows only its own failures, a service's page keeps its own, and leaving the page lets that one go.
- Connecting a service that takes your own key sends Composio the auth scheme under the name its own SDK uses (`authScheme`), so the first custom auth config for a service is created rather than refused. (#46)
- A restart no longer leaves a browser card blank and its browser unusable: the browsers open on the controllers are taken up again where they were (the preview, the live view and taking over work as before), a browser whose session is gone is closed instead of staying as a card that leads nowhere, and the sessions earlier runs left behind are closed on the controllers.
- The status under Amuse's avatar glides to its new size and changes its line with a crossfade, instead of snapping and flickering; the brief "responding" line between two tools no longer flashes in.
- The chat keeps its least width when the window is made narrower after the browser panel was dragged wide; widened again, the panel comes back to the width it was dragged to.
- Amuse can cancel a schedule you no longer want: it looks the schedules up with their full ids instead of going by what it remembered of them.
- On a wider screen, the Amuse avatar flies into its panel and back again when the panel opens and closes.
